## Questions to ask AC explicitly

**Put these in writing and ask for written answers.** Their responses determine your entire sequence — do not begin the bridging work until you know what AC will actually accept.

1. Does the discretionary/exceptional-circumstances admission provision apply to the **DMin**, or only to the **MPhil**? *(Published sources are explicit for MPhil and ambiguous for DMin — this is the most important question in the list.)*
2. What form of evidence would the Graduate Research School Director find persuasive as "equivalent research training"? Specifically: **would a purpose-written critical commentary of 12,000–15,000 words on a published trade book be accepted in lieu of a prior research thesis?**
3. Is **RES701** (or the current equivalent) available as a standalone, non-award enrolment?
4. Is a **peer-reviewed journal article** required, or would a submitted-but-not-yet-accepted article carry weight?
5. What is the required **length and format of the research proposal** for a DMin application?
6. Who in the faculty might **supervise** in this area — practical theology/ecclesiology, and qualitative comparative methods?
7. What are the **intake dates and application deadlines** for 2027?
8. **Fees**, domestic vs international status given Australian citizenship with US residency, and any **scholarship or fee-relief** available to HDR candidates.
9. Can candidature be undertaken **part-time and largely remotely** from the United States, with fieldwork in four countries?
10. Given the insider-research design, what does AC's **Human Research Ethics Committee** expect, and how early can an ethics pre-consultation happen?
11. Does AC provide **library and database access** to prospective candidates preparing an application?

## A note on framing

Do not open with the honorary doctorate, the twenty-one campuses or the book sales. Open with the research question and the fact that you intend to test your own published claims.

Research committees are not moved by scale of ministry — they assess capacity for disciplined inquiry. The most persuasive single sentence available to you is that you are proposing to find out whether you were wrong. Very few applicants can say that credibly, and it will be remembered.
