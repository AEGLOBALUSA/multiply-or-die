# Designing Without the Predecessor Case

**Decision recorded:** the 1970–2000 predecessor period and the 2000 succession are **withdrawn** from the study at the candidate's direction. This document sets out what replaces them — and answers a prior question about what a doctorate can and cannot be.

---

## Part 1 — The question underneath: "isn't the book the thesis?"

You asked whether the book is the thesis, and whether the work is then producing arguments, testimony and transcripts that support it.

**No — and this is the single most important thing in this entire pack.** Not because the instinct is foolish. Because it inverts the one rule a doctorate is built on, and getting it wrong would cost you years.

### What gets a thesis failed

A thesis that gathers evidence **to support** a conclusion the author has already reached is not a thesis. It is advocacy with citations. Examiners are trained to detect exactly this, and it is the most common reason experienced practitioners fail at doctoral level. They arrive certain, and certainty reads as the absence of inquiry.

The test an examiner applies is brutally simple:

> **Could this research have come out the other way?**

If the design makes disconfirmation impossible — if every interview was sought from someone likely to agree, if the analytical frame was chosen after seeing the data, if no finding could have embarrassed the author — then the answer is no, and the work fails regardless of how true its conclusions happen to be.

Note that last clause. **Your propositions may well be right.** That is not the issue. A correct conclusion reached by a method that could not have detected error earns no doctorate, because the method is what is being examined.

### The thing that makes it uncomfortable, and necessary

You have published these claims. You lead the organisation. You appointed the people who would be interviewed. Every one of those facts pushes toward confirmation. That is not a character problem — it is a structural one, and it applies to any insider researcher.

The response is not to pretend the pull isn't there. It is to build a design where **your own preferences cannot reach the result**: the analytical frame fixed and lodged before analysis, interviews conducted by someone else, transcripts anonymised before you see them, negative cases analysed first, and a critical friend whose dissent goes in the thesis.

Those controls are not bureaucracy. They are the reason anyone will believe the findings — including the findings that favour you.

### The legitimate version of your instinct

Here is what is genuinely true in what you said, and it is a lot:

**The book supplies the hypotheses. That is a real and unusual asset.** Most doctoral candidates spend a year working out what to ask. You already have twenty-four formalised propositions (document 08) derived from four decades of practice. That is a serious head start, and it is entirely legitimate.

What changes is the verb. Not:

> ~~The book is the conclusion; the research supports it.~~

But:

> **The book is the prediction; the research tests whether it holds.**

Same book. Same forty years. Same interviews, in many cases the same people. Completely different design, and the difference is whether the answer was fixed before you started.

### And there is a degree shaped like what you described

**PhD by Published Work** exists, and it is close to your instinct: the published work *is* substantially the submission, accompanied by a critical commentary. It is a real route at many universities.

Two problems here. It normally requires **peer-reviewed** outputs, and a trade book is not that. And I have found no evidence AC offers it. If you want, it is worth asking AC directly — but the DMin remains the better target, and it is the one your forty years qualifies you for outright.

## Part 2 — Replacing the predecessor case

Withdrawing the 2000 succession removes the test for **P1** — *addition dies with the leader; multiplication outlives them* — which is the book's central claim. Four ways to get it back. **None of them involve your family.**

### ★ Option 1 — Campus-level successions inside your own network

**This is the strongest replacement, and it is better science than the case you withdrew.**

Twenty-one campuses over twenty-six years have produced a great many leadership transitions — pastors appointed, promoted, moved, departed, removed. Two are already in the book: the campus Janine inherited after the previous pastor left and took people with him; and the Australian campus that passed to Josh Greenwood after the attempted takeover.

Every one of those is a succession event with a before and an after.

| | Predecessor case | Campus successions |
|---|---|---|
| Cases | 1 | ~30–50 |
| Generalisable | No | Yes |
| Family exposure | High | None |
| Data | Pre-2000, patchy | Post-2000, in your archive |

**n=1 could establish mechanism. n=40 can establish pattern.** The proposition is identical — do units built on a leader's own output decline when that leader goes, while units built on leader-production continue? — but it is now testable with real statistical and comparative purchase.

Withdrawing the father case doesn't weaken the study. Done this way, it strengthens it.

### ★ Option 2 — Prospective, pre-registered prediction

**The most credible single thing available to you, and almost nobody can do it.**

*Multiply or Die* was published in 2026. Every campus, plant and hub launched from now on can be **predicted in advance using the book's own framework** — before anyone knows the outcome.

The protocol:

1. Before a new unit launches, score it against the relevant propositions — H.E.A.T. of the leader, 4D coverage of the development team, hub vs. centralised architecture, culture diagnostics, financial model.
2. **Lodge a written prediction with the supervisory panel.** Dated. Sealed. Specific.
3. Launch, and let it run.
4. Compare prediction against outcome over the candidature.

This eliminates hindsight bias entirely, because the prediction pre-dates the result. It is the difference between explaining why something happened and calling it beforehand — and an examiner will recognise instantly that it could have come out the other way.

You are uniquely positioned for it because you control the planting pipeline. Indonesia and Brazil are expanding, and the twelve-hub architecture in Chapter 7 has hubs still forming. Those are your test cases, and they haven't happened yet.

**One warning that must be stated plainly.** This design gives you a live interest in your predictions being right, on decisions affecting real congregations and real leaders. That is a serious conflict and it needs a hard firewall: predictions lodged with the panel, launch decisions made through the normal governance process without reference to the research, and an explicit protocol that the study never influences a planting decision. Put it in the ethics application. An examiner will ask.

### Option 3 — External succession cases

You have access almost no researcher has: the ACC's 1,000+ churches, GSJA in Indonesia, and four decades of relationships across the movement — fourteen years as a national director, ten as a state president.

Study **8–12 other churches** that have been through founder succession. Comparative, multi-case, fully external, and it delivers the counterfactual a single-network study can never supply.

**Cost:** the slowest and most expensive option — negotiated access, more travel, more ethics. **Benefit:** it is the only option that produces findings generalisable beyond your own network, and it removes the insider problem for that component entirely.

### Option 4 — The tracer study

Track every leader the network has developed and sent out since 2000 — wherever they are now, including those who left badly and those who left the ministry altogether.

This measures multiplication rate directly, independent of any single succession, and it surfaces the people least likely to volunteer flattering testimony. **That is exactly why it belongs in the design.** It is also the cleanest test of P20 — *multipliers want freedom, dependents want security* — which predicts your best people left wherever autonomy was constrained.

## Part 3 — Recommended design

**Three levels. No family involvement. Stronger than what it replaces.**

| Level | What | Design | Tests |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | The ~21 units, 2000–2026, full population incl. closures | Retrospective comparative case study + QCA | P2, P5–P11, P16, P17, P19, P22 |
| **B** | ~30–50 campus-level leadership transitions | Retrospective multiple-case succession analysis | **P1**, P12, P13 |
| **C** | New units launched 2027 onward | **Prospective, pre-registered prediction** | Live test of the priority propositions |

Level A is what you already had. **Level B recovers everything the withdrawn case was going to do, with forty times the cases.** Level C is the one that will make a panel sit up, because pre-registered prediction is rare in this field and effectively impossible to fake.

Option 3 stays on the table as an extension if the candidature has room. Option 4 folds into Level B as a data-collection strand.

### What to say when asked why the predecessor period isn't in it

Nothing elaborate, and nothing defensive:

> The study is scoped to the network I have led since 2000, where I hold the complete archive and can access the full population of units including closures. Extending into the preceding generation would have added a single additional case at significant cost to data quality and to people who are not participants in this research.

That is accurate, it is sufficient, and no supervisor will push further.

## Part 4 — What this costs and what it gains

**Lost:** a fifty-six-year time series, and a completed founder-to-successor transition with long follow-up. Real losses. Worth naming honestly in the limitations chapter.

**Gained:**

- **P1 tested across ~40 cases instead of 1** — pattern rather than anecdote
- **A prospective component**, which is the strongest evidence type available and which no retrospective design can match
- **A cleaner ethics application** — no family members, no deceased participants, no contested characterisation of someone who cannot answer
- **A tighter thesis.** Fifty-six years across two generations and four countries was, honestly, a great deal of scope for a part-time candidature. This version is more likely to actually finish — and a finished doctorate beats an ambitious one.
