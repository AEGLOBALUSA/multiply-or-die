## Questions to ask AC explicitly

**Put these in writing and ask for written answers.**

1. **The one that matters most.** AC's DMin page states the entry requirement as a minimum of five years' full-time leadership/ministry experience plus a CV and cover letter. A third-party course listing additionally states that DMin applicants must hold a prior research thesis of at least 12,000 words. **Which is current?** Does any research-output requirement apply to DMin entry beyond the published experience criterion?
2. If a research-capacity test does apply: **would a purpose-written critical commentary of 12,000–15,000 words on a published book satisfy it?**
3. Is **RES701** (or the current equivalent) available as a standalone, non-award enrolment?
4. Would a **submitted-but-not-yet-accepted** journal article carry weight, or is acceptance needed?
5. What is the required **length and format of the research proposal** for a DMin application?
6. Who in the faculty might **supervise** in this area — practical theology/ecclesiology, and qualitative comparative methods?
7. What are the **intake dates and application deadlines** for 2027?
8. **Fees**, domestic vs international status given Australian citizenship with US residency, and any **scholarship or fee relief** for HDR candidates.
9. Can candidature be undertaken **part-time and largely remotely** from the United States, with fieldwork in four countries?
10. Given the insider-research design, what does AC's **Human Research Ethics Committee** expect, and how early can a pre-consultation happen?
11. Does AC provide **library and database access** to prospective candidates preparing an application?

*Questions 1 and 2 govern sequencing. Everything else can be asked in parallel.*

## A note on framing

Do not open with the honorary doctorate, the twenty-one campuses or the book sales. Open with the research question and the fact that you intend to test your own published claims.

Research committees are not moved by scale of ministry — they assess capacity for disciplined inquiry. The most persuasive single sentence available to you is that you are proposing to find out whether you were wrong. Very few applicants can say that credibly, and it will be remembered.
