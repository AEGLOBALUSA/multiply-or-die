## Working title

**Multiplication Architecture: A Practitioner Inquiry into the Conditions of Sustained Church Multiplication in a Transnational Network, 2000–2026**

---

## 1. Abstract

Church multiplication is widely advocated and poorly evidenced. The practitioner literature asserts principles; the empirical literature rarely follows a single network across enough plants, enough countries and enough years to distinguish what transfers from what was circumstantial. This study takes one unusually well-documented case — a transnational network that planted 21+ congregations across four countries between 2000 and 2026, with both flourishing and failed outcomes — and asks which conditions actually distinguish plants that sustain multiplication from those that plateau or close.

The study is deliberately constructed as a **test of assertions the researcher has already published**. The candidate's trade book *Multiply or Die* (2026, ~84,000 words) sets out a series of propositions about multiplication drawn from four decades of practice. Those propositions were derived inductively from experience and have never been systematically examined. This research treats them as **falsifiable hypotheses**, subjects them to comparative case analysis including negative cases, and reports where they hold, where they fail, and where the evidence contradicts them.

The intended contributions are threefold: an empirically grounded model of transferable multiplication conditions; a documented account of multiplication *failure* in a tradition that under-reports it; and a methodological contribution on conducting rigorous insider research in founder-led ecclesial organisations where the researcher holds structural power over participants.

## 2. Background and rationale

### 2.1 The practical problem

Church planting movements invest heavily in multiplication with limited evidence about which factors are causal, which are correlational, and which are artefacts of a particular leader, city or moment. Networks replicate the visible features of successful plants — the service style, the launch strategy, the staffing template — without knowing whether those features carried the outcome. When plants fail, the failures are rarely studied and almost never published, so the field's evidence base is systematically censored toward success.

### 2.2 The researcher's position

I have led Futures Church Global since 2000, growing from a single declining congregation to 21 campuses across Australia, the United States, Indonesia and Brazil. Over the last twelve years the network planted more than twenty-one churches. Most flourished. Some did not. I served fourteen years as a national director across a movement of more than a thousand churches, and ten years as a state president.

This gives me two things no external researcher has: **access** — to twenty-six years of internal records, to the leaders of every plant including those that closed, and to the decision-making rationale behind each — and **a documented prior position**, published as *Multiply or Die*, which can be tested rather than quietly assumed.

It also gives me the study's central methodological problem, addressed in §5.

### 2.3 The gap in the literature

Three bodies of work bear on this and none resolves it:

- **Church growth and church planting literature** is predominantly prescriptive and practitioner-authored, with limited comparative empirical design and a strong survivorship bias.
- **Organisational studies of religious institutions** — organisational ecology, institutional isomorphism, founder-transition research — offer analytical purchase but are rarely applied to multi-site ecclesial networks in the Global South and Global North simultaneously.
- **Leadership pipeline and generative leadership theory** provides transferable constructs for leader development at scale, but has been developed largely in corporate settings and not tested against volunteer-dependent, theologically-motivated organisations.

The gap is a longitudinal, multi-country, single-network comparative study that includes failure cases and is explicit about the theological commitments shaping the organisation studied.

*A full literature review will be developed during candidature; the above indicates the fields to be surveyed, not a finished review.*

## 3. Research questions

**Primary:**

> What organisational, theological and leadership conditions distinguish church plants that achieve sustained multiplication from those that plateau or close, within a single transnational network between 2000 and 2026?

**Subsidiary:**

1. Which of the propositions advanced in *Multiply or Die* are supported by systematic comparative evidence across the network's plants, and which are not?
2. How do the conditions for sustained multiplication differ across national contexts (Australia, United States, Indonesia, Brazil), and what does that variation reveal about which conditions are transferable versus context-dependent?
3. What role does leader-development architecture — the pipeline by which a plant produces its next generation of leaders — play in distinguishing sustained multiplication from plateau?
4. What can be learned from the network's failed and closed plants that its successful plants cannot show?
5. How does founder-centrality function as both an enabler and a constraint on multiplication over a twenty-six-year horizon?

## 4. Conceptual framework

The study brings three lenses to bear:

**Organisational ecology and institutional theory** — treating each plant as an organisation in an environment, attending to founding conditions, resource dependence, and the density of the local ecclesial field. This supplies the vocabulary for asking why identical strategies produced divergent outcomes in Adelaide, Alpharetta, Bogor and São Paulo.

**Leadership pipeline / generative leadership theory** — treating multiplication as fundamentally a *leader-production* problem rather than a *congregation-production* problem. This directly operationalises the book's core claim that addition dies with the leader while multiplication compounds.

**Theology of multiplication** — the study's organisation is theologically motivated and its decisions are theologically justified; a framework that brackets this would misdescribe the case. The Genesis mandate, apostolic sending patterns, and the theology of generational succession are treated as *live causal factors in participants' reasoning*, analysed as such rather than assumed true or dismissed.

The three lenses are held in tension deliberately. Where the organisational and theological readings of the same event diverge, that divergence is data.

## 5. Methodology

### 5.1 Design

**Retrospective, longitudinal, comparative multiple-case study** using insider practitioner research, with an embedded mixed-methods component.

Unit of analysis: the individual church plant (n ≈ 21–25, including closed plants).

Case selection: **the full population**, not a sample. Every plant initiated by the network between 2000 and 2026 is included, whether it flourished, plateaued, merged or closed. Including the full population is what removes survivorship bias, and it is only possible because of insider access.

### 5.2 Data sources

| Source | Description |
|---|---|
| **Archival quantitative** | Attendance, giving, volunteer counts, staff counts, leaders-produced-and-sent, plant-to-plant lineage, by plant by year, 2000–2026 |
| **Archival qualitative** | Board minutes, planting proposals, launch plans, post-mortems, internal correspondence |
| **Semi-structured interviews** | Lead/campus pastors of every plant (target n ≈ 30–40), including those of closed plants and those who have since left the network |
| **Documentary** | *Multiply or Die* itself, treated as a primary source documenting the researcher's prior position |

The inclusion of **leaders who have left the network, on poor terms as well as good**, is essential and non-negotiable. A study of this network that interviews only its current staff would produce exactly the flattery this field already suffers from.

### 5.3 Analysis

- **Within-case:** chronological reconstruction of each plant from founding conditions to current state.
- **Cross-case:** structured comparison on a pre-registered set of conditions, using qualitative comparative analysis (QCA) to identify which combinations of conditions are necessary and/or sufficient for sustained multiplication.
- **Proposition testing:** each testable proposition extracted from *Multiply or Die* is stated formally, operationalised, and assessed against the cross-case evidence. **The analytical framework and the propositions are fixed before analysis begins** and lodged with the supervisory panel, so that post-hoc rationalisation is visible if it occurs.
- **Negative case analysis:** closed and plateaued plants are analysed first, before the successful ones, to counter the pull toward confirmatory reading.

### 5.4 The insider problem — and what is done about it

I am the Global Senior Pastor of the organisation under study. I appointed most of the participants. I made many of the decisions being evaluated. I have published a book asserting the conclusions this study is meant to test. Each of these is a threat to validity, and together they are the defining methodological challenge of the project.

This is not a reason to abandon the study — insider practitioner research is an established tradition, and the access it affords is precisely what makes the research possible. It is a reason to build the controls in from the beginning:

1. **Independent recruitment and interviewing.** Participants are recruited and interviewed by an independent researcher, not by me. I do not know who declined.
2. **Anonymised transcripts.** I receive de-identified transcripts only. Identifying detail is stripped by the independent researcher before I see it.
3. **An independent gatekeeper** with authority to exclude any participant, question or finding on welfare grounds, and to halt the study.
4. **A critical friend** external to the network — ideally a scholar with no relationship to the movement — who reviews analysis for confirmatory drift and whose dissent is recorded in the thesis.
5. **Reflexive journal**, maintained throughout and reported in the methodology chapter, documenting where my prior commitments were challenged and how I responded.
6. **Disconfirmation as a success criterion.** The study is designed so that finding my published propositions wrong is a successful outcome, not a failed one. This is stated in the proposal, in the ethics application, and to participants.

### 5.5 Ethics

Formal ethics approval will be sought from AC's Human Research Ethics Committee prior to any data collection. Principal concerns:

- **Power asymmetry.** Staff cannot freely decline a request from their Global Senior Pastor. Addressed by §5.4(1)–(3), plus explicit written assurance that participation, non-participation and withdrawal have no employment consequence, communicated by the independent gatekeeper rather than by me.
- **Participants who left under difficult circumstances.** These interviews carry the highest risk of harm and the highest evidential value. Independent conduct, right of withdrawal at any point up to submission, and review of their own quoted material before publication.
- **Confidentiality within a small, identifiable population.** With ~21 plants, standard anonymisation is insufficient — a named country plus a year identifies a person. Requires composite reporting and, in some cases, agreed suppression of detail.
- **Organisational reputational risk.** Findings may be unflattering to the network I lead. The gatekeeper arrangement and the supervisory panel exist partly so that the decision to publish an uncomfortable finding is not mine alone.

## 6. Anticipated contributions

**To knowledge:** A population-complete, twenty-six-year, four-country comparative account of church multiplication that includes failures — a dataset and analysis with, to my knowledge, no close equivalent.

**To practice:** A model of multiplication conditions distinguishing the transferable from the context-dependent, usable by networks other than my own.

**To method:** A worked, critically examined protocol for insider practitioner research in founder-led religious organisations — a situation common in this field and rarely handled well.

**To the tradition:** A documented, publishable account of multiplication failure, in a movement whose evidence base is distorted by the reluctance to report it.
