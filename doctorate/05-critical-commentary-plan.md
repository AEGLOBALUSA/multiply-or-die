# Converting *Multiply or Die* into a Research Output

**Probably not required for DMin entry. Do it anyway — it is a year of thesis work banked early.**

---

## 1. Why this document exists

On AC's published criteria, DMin entry turns on **five years' full-time ministry experience** — which you exceed eightfold — not on a prior research output. So read this document in the right register: it is **insurance and head start**, not a hurdle.

It earns its place three ways:

1. **If AC does apply a research-capacity test** (see the unresolved conflict in document 01 §1), this answers it completely and pre-emptively.
2. **It is the strongest possible differentiator** in an application that otherwise rests on experience alone. Experience gets you admitted; demonstrated capacity for disciplined inquiry gets you a good supervisor and a confident panel at confirmation of candidature.
3. **It is thesis work you will do regardless.** The literature survey, the formalisation of your propositions, and the honest statement of the book's limits are chapters two and eight of the thesis in draft. Doing them now compresses year one.

The instrument is a **critical commentary**: a scholarly document standing alongside a published work, doing for it what the work itself never had to do — stating method, engaging literature, and making a bounded claim about what is contributed and what is merely asserted. It is the standard, recognised instrument in *PhD by Published Work* applications internationally.

---

## 2. What it must do

A critical commentary that works has to be genuinely self-critical. A 12,000-word restatement of the book's argument in more formal language will fail, and will fail worse than submitting nothing, because it demonstrates that you cannot distinguish assertion from evidence — the exact capacity in question.

It must:

1. **State the book's propositions formally.** Extract the claims from twenty chapters of narrative and render each as a proposition that could, in principle, be shown false.
2. **Classify the evidential basis of each.** Honestly. Most will be *practitioner observation* — a category with real value and real limits. Some will be *scriptural-theological argument*. Very few will be *systematically evidenced*. Saying so is the point.
3. **Situate the work in the literature.** Where does *Multiply or Die* sit relative to the church-growth literature, the organisational studies literature, the leadership-pipeline literature? What does it say that others have said? Where does it depart, and on what basis?
4. **Identify the contribution.** What, specifically, does this book add that was not already available? Be narrow and defensible. One genuine contribution stated precisely beats five stated broadly.
5. **State the limitations.** Single-network, founder-authored, retrospective, survivorship-weighted, no external validation. Name every one before an examiner does.
6. **Bridge to the proposed research.** Show how the identified limitations generate the research questions in document 02. This is what turns the commentary from a defence of past work into a warrant for future work.

## 3. Proposed structure (12,000–15,000 words)

| § | Section | Words | Content |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Introduction and purpose | 800 | What the book is, what the commentary is for, what it claims and does not claim |
| 2 | Context of production | 1,200 | The twenty-six-year practice base; how the propositions were arrived at; the book's intended audience and genre, and why genre matters for evidential standards |
| 3 | The propositions | 3,000 | Formal statement of the book's core claims, organised thematically. **✅ Drafted — see document 08** |

| 7 | Limitations | 1,200 | Comprehensive and unflinching |
| 8 | From assertion to inquiry | 1,600 | How §7 generates the research questions in the proposal |

## 4. The peer-reviewed article

Run this in parallel. One submitted article materially strengthens the discretionary case; one *accepted* article changes the conversation entirely, because it converts "evidence of research capacity" from a judgement call into a fact.

**Target journal:** *Australasian Pentecostal Studies* — AC's own journal. Editorially sympathetic to the tradition, familiar with practitioner-scholars, and a publication there is legible to the people assessing your application.

**Proposed article:** Draw on the strongest single case in the book — the **closed church plant** (Chapter 2). It is the most academically interesting material you have, precisely because it is the failure. An honest, analytically framed account of why a well-resourced plant with a committed leader failed, written by the person who made the decision that caused it, is publishable and rare.

Working title: *"Planting Against the Blueprint: A Retrospective Case Analysis of Church Plant Failure in an Australian Multi-Site Network."*

**Why the failure case rather than a success case:** it demonstrates the disposition the whole application depends on — that you will report findings against your own interest. Leading with it is strategically strong and intellectually honest at the same time.

## 5. Research methods training

AC requires demonstrated research methodology knowledge equivalent to **RES701**. Options, in order of preference:

1. **Enrol in RES701 (or AC's current equivalent) as a single non-award unit.** Cleanest possible answer — it removes the question rather than arguing it. Ask AC whether this is available as a standalone enrolment.
2. Complete an equivalent postgraduate research methods unit elsewhere and seek recognition.
3. Argue equivalence from prior work — **weakest option**; avoid if either of the above is available.

## 7. Get help with the literature — but not with the argument

The literature survey is the part of this that is genuinely slow and genuinely skilled, and it is legitimate to get research assistance for locating and summarising sources. Two hard limits:

- **The argument, the classification of your own claims, and the limitations must be yours.** Those sections are the assessment. Outsourcing them defeats the purpose and is detectable.
- **Everything cited must be read.** A commentary with a citation the author cannot discuss in a viva is worse than a commentary with a thinner bibliography.

---

## 8. What success looks like

At the end of this process you hold:

- A 12,000–15,000-word critical commentary meeting the ≥12,000-word threshold in substance and in form
- A submitted (ideally accepted) peer-reviewed article
- A completed research methods unit
- An 84,000-word published book as supporting evidence of sustained engagement
- Forty years of practice against a five-year requirement

At that point the discretionary decision is straightforward for the Graduate Research School Director to make in your favour — which is the entire objective. You are not asking them to bend a rule. You are handing them the evidence the rule asks for.
