**Re: Application for admission — Doctor of Ministry**

I am writing to apply for admission to the Doctor of Ministry, and to set out how I meet the programme's entry requirement of a minimum of five years' full-time leadership and ministry experience.

I have been in full-time ministry for approximately **forty years**. I began preaching at nineteen. Since 2000 I have led Futures Church Global, taking a single declining congregation in Adelaide and building it into a network of **twenty-one campuses across four countries** — Australia, the United States, Indonesia and Brazil. In the last twelve years we have planted more than twenty-one churches. Alongside that I served **fourteen years as a national director** across a movement of more than a thousand churches, and **ten years as a state president**. I hold a bachelor's degree, and Alphacrucis conferred an honorary doctorate on me in recognition of this work — which I mention as recognition rather than as academic standing, and on which I make no claim for admission purposes.

That is the experience criterion. What I want to use it for is more specific.

In 2026 I published *Multiply or Die*, an 84,000-word book setting out what four decades of practice have taught me about multiplication. It has been well received. It is also, by the standards that matter here, unfinished work: it is a trade book, it states no methodology, and it does not test a single one of its own claims. **I am applying because I want to test them.**

I am in an unusual position to do so, for two reasons.

The first is access. I hold a complete organisational archive covering every church plant the network has initiated since 2000, across four national contexts — including the plants that plateaued and the one that closed, which closed as a direct result of a decision I made against our own stated criteria. Church planting's evidence base is systematically distorted by the reluctance to study failure. A comparative study of the **full population** of a network's plants, including its failures, would address a real gap.

The second is generational. **This organisation did not begin with me.** My father led it from 1970 to 2000 — Klemzig, then Paradise from 1982 — growing it from roughly 150 to over 2,000, before handing it to Jane and me and turning to public life. His father, born in Wales in 1901, was a coal miner who became a missionary to India and then to Papua New Guinea, where some three thousand churches trace their origins to that work. My three sons are in ministry now.

That gives the study something a single-generation case cannot offer: **a completed founder-to-successor transition with twenty-six years of follow-up data**, inside the case and in the archive. It is what makes the book's central claim — that addition dies with the leader while multiplication outlives them — testable rather than merely asserted. It also lets me examine the succession propositions I published from the side the literature almost never hears from: not the founder handing over, but the successor receiving.

My proposal therefore treats the propositions I published as **falsifiable hypotheses rather than conclusions**, and is designed so that disconfirming them counts as a successful outcome.

I am equally clear that being the founder, the decision-maker and the published advocate makes me the study's central methodological problem. Insider practitioner research is an established tradition, and the access it affords is what makes this project possible — but it demands controls built in from the start, not bolted on. My proposal specifies independent recruitment and interviewing, anonymised transcripts, an independent gatekeeper with authority to halt the study, and an external critical friend whose dissent is recorded in the thesis.

To demonstrate readiness for doctoral-level inquiry I am also preparing, and will complete prior to or early in candidature:

- a **critical commentary** on *Multiply or Die* of 12,000–15,000 words, situating the book within the church multiplication, organisational studies and leadership development literatures, classifying the evidential basis of each of its propositions, and stating its limitations;
- a **peer-reviewed journal article** on the network's documented church plant failure, for submission to *Australasian Pentecostal Studies*;
- **RES701** or an equivalent research methodology unit.

I would welcome a conversation about the proposal and about supervision. If the Graduate Research School considers a different entry point more appropriate, I would rather hear that and begin where the evidence supports than push for the faster route.

My connection to Alphacrucis is long-standing — Futures College operates as a delivery partner, and I hold the University's honorary doctorate. I would value doing this work within an institution whose tradition and commitments I share, and whose faculty understand from the inside the context I am researching.

Thank you for your consideration.

Yours sincerely,

**Ashley Mark Evans**
Global Senior Pastor, Futures Church Global

## Note on what this letter does

It leads with the criterion AC actually publishes — experience — and satisfies it in the first paragraph. Everything after that is not qualification, it is *differentiation*: the research question, the archive, and the willingness to be proved wrong.

The honorary doctorate is mentioned once, in a subordinate clause, and immediately characterised correctly. That is deliberate. Handled that way it works in your favour — AC has already judged your contribution doctoral-calibre — without ever looking like a claim on entry.
