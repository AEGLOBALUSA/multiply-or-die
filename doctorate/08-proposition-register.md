# The Proposition Register

**Extracted from *Multiply or Die* (2026), 20 chapters + introduction, ~84,000 words**

This is the analytical spine of both the critical commentary (document 05, §3–4) and chapter 8 of the thesis. It does the work that turns a trade book into a research object: it states each claim formally, classifies what kind of claim it is, and says what would show it to be wrong.

**Do not skip the classification column.** Its honesty is the point. A register that graded every proposition "empirically testable" would be worthless — and transparently so to any examiner. The genuine finding here is that **of 24 propositions, 16 are directly testable against the case, 5 require comparison outside it, and 3 are theological rather than empirical — while nine of the book's most memorable devices carry no independent evidential weight at all.** Saying that plainly is what demonstrates research capacity.

> **Revision note.** P1 — the book's central claim — is class A, tested across the **thirty to fifty campus-level leadership transitions** the network has generated since 2000. An earlier revision routed it through the 2000 handover from the candidate's predecessor; that case has been withdrawn from the study (document 09). The unit-level route is the stronger of the two: a single succession could only illustrate mechanism, whereas thirty to fifty can establish pattern.

---

## Classification scheme

| Code | Class | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | Empirically testable, internal | Can be operationalised against the network's 2000–2026 archive and interview data |
| **B** | Testable, needs external comparison | Requires data from outside the network; the single-case design cannot settle it alone |
| **C** | Theological / normative | Not empirically falsifiable. Assessed by exegetical and theological argument, and analysed as a live causal factor in participants' reasoning |
| **D** | Illustrative / analogical | Rhetorical devices carrying no independent evidential weight. Legitimate as exposition; inadmissible as evidence |
| **E** | Definitional | True by construction as stated. Requires restatement before it can be tested |

## Cluster 1 — The core thesis

### P1 · Addition dies with the leader; multiplication outlives them
*Introduction*

> **As stated:** "Addition dies when you die. But multiplication? Multiplication outlives you."

**Formal:** Organisations whose growth depends on the leader's direct output decline following that leader's departure; organisations whose growth depends on leader-production continue growing after it.

**Class: A — via unit-level succession.** *Revised twice. Originally graded B on the assumption the claim needed a founder succession the network hadn't had. Then reclassified to A on the basis of the 2000 handover from the candidate's predecessor. **That case has since been withdrawn from the study at the candidate's direction** (see document 09). P1 remains class A — tested instead across the thirty to fifty campus-level leadership transitions the network has generated since 2000.*

**Operationalisation:** For each transition, code the outgoing leader's operating mode — the share of the unit's growth attributable to that leader's own direct output (preaching draw, personal pastoral reach, local reputation) versus to leader-production. Then interrupted time series on attendance, giving and leaders-produced across the transition, comparing outcomes at 24 and 60 months, controlling for unit size, age and context.

**P1 predicts:** the higher the addition-share of a unit's pre-transition growth, the sharper the post-transition decline.

**Falsified by:** Addition-built units transferring without discontinuity; or leader-producing units collapsing at transition.

**Why this is stronger than the withdrawn case.** A single founder succession could establish mechanism and could falsify, but could never generalise. Thirty to fifty transitions can establish *pattern* — with statistical purchase, controls, and variation in circumstance (planned handovers, promotions, resignations, removals, departures under conflict) that a single case cannot supply. The withdrawal cost the study a long time series and gained it a real n. On balance the proposition is now better tested, not worse.

**Live test available.** P1 is also a Level C candidate: for units launched from 2027, the addition/multiplication profile can be scored *before* any transition occurs, and the prediction lodged in advance.

### P2 · The leader is the ceiling
*Chapter 1*

> **As stated:** "Addition has a ceiling. That ceiling is you."

**Formal:** Where a leader retains direct operational responsibility across functions, organisational growth plateaus at a level determined by that leader's personal capacity rather than by market, resource or contextual factors.

**Class: A.** Strong candidate for the study's central empirical test.

**Operationalisation:** For each plant, code the lead pastor's span of direct operational responsibility by year (preaching, counselling, administration, worship, finance). Test against attendance and giving trajectories, controlling for local population growth and ecclesial density.

**Falsified by:** Plants with high founder-centrality showing growth trajectories indistinguishable from plants with distributed responsibility.

**Note:** This is the book's most falsifiable and most interesting claim. It is also the one most likely to be confounded — high-capacity leaders may both retain more responsibility *and* attract more growth. The analysis must separate the two.

### P3 · God measures multiplication rate, not total output
*Chapter 6*

> **As stated:** "Your assignment isn't to reach the most people. It's to multiply at the highest rate."

**Formal:** Multiplication rate (leaders produced and sent per leader per period) is a better predictor of long-run organisational persistence than aggregate size at any point.

**Class: A** for the empirical half; **C** for the normative half ("God measures").

**Operationalisation:** Compute leaders-sent-per-leader-per-year for each plant. Regress 10-year persistence and daughter-plant counts on both multiplication rate and peak attendance. The claim predicts rate dominates.

**Falsified by:** Peak size predicting persistence better than rate.

**Note:** The Whitefield/Wesley contrast (Ch. 6) is **class D** — a historical illustration, not evidence. It is rhetorically powerful and evidentially inert. The commentary must say so.

## Cluster 2 — Selecting multipliers

### P4 · Experienced leaders are systematically blind to potential
*Chapter 3*

> **As stated:** "The leaders who should possess the sharpest vision for potential are often the most blind to it."

**Formal:** Leader-identification accuracy declines with tenure, via four mechanisms: past-tense vision, list-based vision, formula-based vision, impatient vision.

**Class: A — via Level C.** *Previously graded B and written off as untestable within a six-year candidature, because it requires recording predictions before outcomes are known. **The prospective component makes it testable.*** Retrospective assessment remains fatally contaminated by hindsight and should not be attempted.

**Operationalisation:** At the point of each new leadership appointment, the candidate and a panel of long-tenured leaders independently record predictions about the appointee's development trajectory, alongside predictions from leaders with shorter tenure. Predictions are lodged and sealed. Outcomes are compared at 24 and 48 months. P4 predicts that **accuracy declines with predictor tenure**.

**Falsified by:** Long-tenured predictors matching or outperforming shorter-tenured ones.

**Note:** This one directly implicates the researcher's own judgement — he is among the longest-tenured predictors in the sample. A design in which the author's own selection accuracy is measured against his juniors, on record, in advance, is an unusually strong signal of good faith. It is also the proposition most likely to produce a genuinely surprising result.

### P5 · Invest disproportionately in the already-fertile (H.E.A.T.)
*Chapter 5*

> **As stated:** "Find the ones in H.E.A.T. Hungry, Effective, Adaptable, Transferable... Stop trying to warm up cold sheep."

**Formal:** Concentrating development resource on individuals scoring high on hunger, effectiveness, adaptability and transferability produces greater leader-yield than distributing it evenly.

**Class: A.** Testable, and among the study's most practically useful questions.

**Operationalisation:** Retrospectively score developed leaders on the four H.E.A.T. dimensions from documentary and interview evidence; correlate with subsequent leader-production. Blind the scoring — the independent researcher scores, not you.

**Falsified by:** No relationship between H.E.A.T. scores and downstream leader-yield, or evenly-distributed investment matching concentrated investment.

**Note:** Jacob's speckled flocks (Gen. 30) is **class D**. The agricultural mechanism does not transfer; the selection principle may. Do not let the illustration do argumentative work.

---

### P6 · Character predicts multiplication better than talent
*Chapter 2, Grave 1*

**Formal:** Among developed leaders, character indicators (teachability, transparency, loyalty under pressure) predict long-run multiplication outcomes better than competence indicators.

**Class: A.** Testable, with a serious measurement problem: "character" is assessed retrospectively by the person who was betrayed. Requires blinded coding against contemporaneous records — appraisals, correspondence, minutes — not present-day recollection.

## Cluster 3 — Developing multipliers

### P7 · Training multiplies; teaching does not
*Chapter 13*

> **As stated:** "Classroom fills heads. Laboratory changes lives."

**Formal:** Leader development involving supervised practice, real assignments with real consequences, and public commissioning (the 5 I's / P.A.I.D. system) produces higher leader-yield than content transmission alone.

**Class: A.** One of the register's strongest candidates.

**Operationalisation:** Code each plant's development method by year. Compare leader-yield across methods, controlling for plant size and leader tenure.

---

### P8 · Development capacity is bounded at roughly twelve
*Chapters 7, 13*

> **As stated:** "You can barely train twelve."

**Formal:** Per-leader development effectiveness declines sharply above approximately twelve direct developmental relationships.

**Class: A**, and unusually clean — it makes a specific numerical prediction, which is rare in this literature and very attractive to examine.

**Falsified by:** No inflection in effectiveness around twelve, or an inflection at a materially different number.

**Note:** The biblical numerology supporting twelve (Ch. 7 — twelve tribes, twelve gates, 144 cubits) is **class D**. If an inflection exists it will be a cognitive-load finding, not a scriptural one. Conflating the two would be the single most damaging move available to this thesis; the commentary should name the temptation explicitly.

---

### P9 · The 4D Protocol is a necessary sequence
*Chapter 9*

> **As stated:** Discover → Develop → Deploy → Depart. "Most leaders discover potential but never develop it. They develop talent but never deploy it. They deploy but can't depart."

**Formal:** All four stages are necessary for multiplication; omission of any one halts it at a predictable point.

**Class: A**, and well-suited to qualitative comparative analysis, which is built precisely for necessity/sufficiency claims about combinations of conditions.

**Operationalisation:** Code each leader-development sequence for presence/absence of each D. QCA for necessary and sufficient combinations.

---

### P10 · No single leader covers all four Ds
*Chapter 9*

**Formal:** Individual leaders show consistent strength/weakness profiles across the four stages; multiplication requires a team covering all four.

**Class: A.** Testable and, if supported, among the most practically transferable findings available.

## Cluster 4 — Deployment, departure, succession

### P11 · Movement builders fail by staying too long
*Chapter 16*

> **As stated:** "Movement-building leaders don't fail by quitting too early. They fail by staying too long in one assignment."

**Formal:** Leader tenure in a single assignment beyond an optimal window is negatively associated with subsequent multiplication in that unit.

**Class: A.** Directly testable — tenure and outcomes are both in the archive.

**Operationalisation:** Model leader tenure against unit-level multiplication outcomes; test for the predicted inverted-U.

**Note:** The "five-year cycle" claim is autobiographical (n=1) and therefore **class D as evidence**, though it is a legitimate hypothesis generator. The register must not launder the author's personal pattern into a general law — this is exactly the slippage a critical commentary exists to catch.

---

### P12 · Succession requires transferring all five batons
*Chapter 19*

> **As stated:** Title, Responsibility, Authority, Relationships, Vision. "Most failed transitions transfer one or two. All five must transfer."

**Formal:** Successful leadership transitions transfer all five elements; failed transitions transfer a subset.

**Class: A.** Excellent proposition — specific, decomposable, and now testable at two levels.

**Operationalisation:** Code every campus-level leadership transition, 1970–2026, for transfer of each baton; compare against post-transition outcomes at 24 and 60 months.

**Why this is unusual:** the succession literature is written overwhelmingly from the departing leader's perspective, and almost always about a single high-profile handover. A multi-case analysis of thirty to fifty ordinary transitions — promotions, relocations, resignations, removals, departures under conflict — inside one network is a different and rarer object of study. The unglamorous cases are where the pattern actually lives.

---

### P13 · Pass the baton at full speed, not at exhaustion
*Chapter 19*

**Formal:** Transitions initiated from strength (high momentum, incumbent healthy) outperform transitions initiated from crisis, exhaustion, or forced circumstance.

**Class: A.** Testable using the same transition dataset as P12.

## Cluster 5 — Architecture and governance

### P14 · Castle high vs kingdom wide
*Chapter 7*

> **As stated:** "Don't build a castle. Build a kingdom. The castle collapses. The kingdom multiplies."

**Formal:** Distributed multi-hub architectures produce higher long-run multiplication than centralised single-centre architectures.

**Class: B.** The network is a single case that chose one architecture. Internal data can trace *how* the shift to hubs affected multiplication, but cannot establish the counterfactual.

**Note:** WeWork (Ch. 7) is **class D** — a business anecdote from a different sector with different failure mechanisms. It illustrates; it does not evidence.

---

### P15 · Two hubs, not one
*Chapter 7*

> **As stated:** "One hub is a bottleneck. Two hubs create distributed capacity."

**Formal:** Organisations establishing a second hub before scaling show higher subsequent multiplication than those scaling from a single centre.

**Class: B**, but with an internal component: the network's own transition from one hub to two is a documented before/after within the archive. That makes it an interrupted time series — weaker than a controlled comparison, considerably stronger than assertion.

---

### P16 · Boundaries beat permissions (the Five Fences)
*Chapters 16, 20*

> **As stated:** "When you lead by permission slips, you can only move as fast as your slowest approval process. When you lead by boundaries, speed is the default."

**Formal:** Governance defining a small number of explicit prohibitions, with full discretion inside them, produces faster decision cycles and higher multiplication than approval-based governance.

**Class: A.** The network adopted a Carver-model governance structure at a datable point. Decision latency and multiplication rate before and after are both recoverable.

**Note:** The book's own caveat — "the Carver Model runs on trust... if you don't have trust, don't start here" — is a **scope condition**, and a well-observed one. It should be formalised as a moderator variable, not dropped. Its presence in the book is a point in the author's favour and worth noting in the commentary.

---

### P17 · Hold DNA tight, execution loose
*Chapter 7*

**Formal:** Multi-site networks enforcing tight ideological/cultural conformity with loose operational conformity outperform those enforcing either both or neither.

**Class: A** internally (variation exists across the four countries), **B** for the general claim.

**Operationalisation:** This is where the four-country design earns its keep. Indonesia, Brazil, the US and Australia differ substantially in operational execution while sharing stated DNA — a natural comparison.

---

### P18 · Territorial thinking caps multiplication
*Chapter 18*

**Formal:** Networks conceiving themselves as one church across territories multiply faster than those conceiving themselves as a church in a place.

**Class: B.** The network holds only its own self-conception; the comparison is external.

## Cluster 6 — Culture and environment

### P19 · Soil beats strategy
*Chapter 14*

> **As stated:** "You can have the right strategy, clearest vision, best people... But if you have the wrong ENVIRONMENT? Nothing multiplies."

**Formal:** Organisational culture — measured on celebration of departures, response to new ideas, debrief-vs-punish after failure, and reward horizon — predicts multiplication outcomes more strongly than stated strategy.

**Class: A.** The four diagnostic questions in Ch. 14 are unusually well-formed and convert almost directly into a coding instrument. This is the register's most immediately operationalisable proposition.

**Operationalisation:** Code each plant's culture on the four dimensions from interviews and documents; compare explanatory power against strategy-fidelity measures.

**Note:** The tearless-onion story is **class D**. It is a memorable illustration of environmental determination and evidence of nothing.

---

### P20 · Multipliers want freedom; dependents want security
*Chapter 14*

**Formal:** High-potential leaders exit organisations offering security without autonomy at higher rates than lower-potential leaders.

**Class: A**, and testable in a way that is genuinely uncomfortable — it predicts that the network's *own* departures should skew toward its highest-potential people wherever autonomy was constrained. Testing it honestly means examining departures you may prefer to explain differently.

**Note:** Flag this one to the supervisory panel early. It is the proposition where insider bias will bite hardest, and pre-committing to its analysis is a strong signal of good faith.

---

## Cluster 7 — Sustainability

### P21 · Family resentment forecloses ministry multiplication
*Chapter 11*

**Formal:** Leaders whose children experience ministry as extraction rather than participation show lower long-run multiplication, mediated by generational discontinuity.

**Class: B**, and **ethically constrained**. Testing it means interviewing leaders' adult children about their upbringing. Within a network the researcher leads, that is a serious undertaking with real potential for harm. **Recommendation: exclude from the thesis.** Note it as a hypothesis; do not pursue it under this candidature.

---

### P22 · Financial architecture gates multiplication
*Chapter 17*

> **As stated:** "Your building is either an engine or an anchor."

**Formal:** Plants whose facilities generate revenue sufficient to substantially offset their own cost multiply faster than those where facilities are net drains.

**Class: A.** Property and financial data are in the archive across all four countries. Clean, unglamorous, and likely to yield a real result.

---

### P23 · Operating from grace, not works, prevents burnout
*Chapter 15*

**Formal:** Leaders operating from a theology of received sufficiency show lower burnout and longer effective tenure than those operating from performance.

**Class: C** as stated — the mechanism is theological. **Class B** if reduced to a psychological construct via validated burnout instruments, but that reduction changes the claim into something the author did not make. The commentary should be explicit about this, because it is a good worked example of the difference between a theological claim and an empirical proxy for one.

## Cluster 8 — Theological foundations

### P24 · Multiplication is the created order (the Genesis mandate)
*Chapters 4, 8*

> **As stated:** "God's first command wasn't moral—it was mathematical."

**Class: C.** Not empirically falsifiable and not offered as such. Assessed by exegetical argument, and analysed in the thesis as a **live causal factor in participants' reasoning** — leaders in this network make decisions because they believe this, and that belief is data about behaviour whether or not the claim is true.

**Note:** The cell-biology material in Ch. 4 (bone marrow, cell division) and the barrenness pattern in Ch. 8 are **class D** — analogical, not evidential. Genesis 1:28 does not become empirically supported because cells divide. The commentary must say so directly; an examiner will spot the equivocation immediately if it is left standing, and dealing with it pre-emptively converts a vulnerability into a credit.

## Summary

| Class | Count | Propositions |
|---|---|---|
| **A** — testable internally | 16 | **P1**, P2, **P4**, P5, P6, P7, P8, P9, P10, P11, P12, P13, P16, P19, P20, P22 |
| **A/C mixed** | 2 | P3 (rate-predicts-persistence is A; "God measures" is C) · P17 (internal variation is A; general claim is B) |
| **B** — needs external comparison | 5 | P14, P15, P18, P21, P23 (only if reduced to a psychological proxy) |
| **C** — theological | 3 | P23 as stated, P24, and the normative half of P3 |
| **D** — illustrative only | 9 devices | Whitefield/Wesley · Jacob's flocks · WeWork · tearless onions · bamboo · penny-doubling · cell division · biblical numerology · the five-year cycle |
| **E** — definitional as worded | 2 | Elements of P1 and P3 require restatement before they can be tested |

**What this tells you, and what to do with it:**

1. **The book's central claim (P1) is testable — across unit-level successions.** The decisive analytical question is whether a departing leader's unit was addition-built or multiplication-built, and what the transition therefore reveals. With thirty to fifty transitions this yields pattern rather than anecdote, and P1 is additionally available for prospective testing at Level C.

2. **The thesis should concentrate on the sixteen class-A propositions**, and P1, P2, P9, P12, P16 and P19 are the strongest six. That is more than enough for a doctoral contribution and considerably more than most single-site studies can attempt.

3. **The class-D material is the book's rhetorical engine and must be quarantined from the evidence.** Naming this yourself is one of the most persuasive things the commentary can do.

4. **P20 and P21 are where the ethics bite.** P20 should be pursued with the panel's oversight; P21 should be dropped.
